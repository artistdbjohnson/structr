## 1. What this is (and why skill practice)

Dumbbells are the most common free weight people actually own. Press, row, and goblet squat aren’t “random arm day” — they’re **patterns** you groove until load can climb without form falling apart.

Structr’s dumbbell wedge is built as **practice sessions**, not smash-to-failure workouts. Conditioning and muscle show up as byproducts of clean, repeatable sets. Same five-phase path as kettlebell:

**Warm-up → Skill drills → Form → THE BULK → Cool-down**

**What we ship in this module**

| Template | Skill spine |
|----------|-------------|
| **Goblet Squat Path** | Front-loaded squat → honest depth · upright torso |
| **Press Path** | Floor or bench press · packed shoulder · soft lock |
| **Row Path** | Hinge brace → elbow to hip · quiet torso |

Info page job: orient people so they pick a template with eyes open, know why RPE shows up on The Bulk, and stop guessing about which pair of bells to buy first.

> **Light safety note:** If something hurts sharp (not “working hard”), park the bells. Film a set or find a coach when you can. One line is enough.

## 2. Safety + common mistakes

### Goblet squat

**Blueprint:** Hold one dumbbell vertical at the chest (hands under the top head). Sit between the hips; heels stay down; knees chase the toes. Not a forward fold. Not a shallow knee bend.

**Common misses**

| Mistake | What it looks like | Better cue |
|---------|-------------------|------------|
| Forward lean | Chest tips; elbows drift out | Elbows **down**; chest proud; bell stays glued to sternum |
| Heel lift | Toes take over at the bottom | Widen stance a touch; or temporary heel plates — then own flat feet |
| Knee cave | Knees dive inward out of the hole | “Knees chase little toes”; soft push-out on the way down |
| Cutting depth | Stops well above parallel | Hip crease below top of knee when it feels honest |
| Breath dump | Exhale halfway down; torso collapses | Big belly breath **before** you sit; hold brace through the hole |

**Practice note:** The front load is a teacher — it punishes tipping. Film from the side every couple of weeks; eyes lie about depth.

### Dumbbell press (floor or bench)

**Blueprint:** Shoulder blades set; ribs quiet; elbows ~45° from the torso — not a “T” flare. Soft finish at the top, not a hard elbow slam.

**Common misses**

| Mistake | What it looks like | Better cue |
|---------|-------------------|------------|
| Elbow flare | Elbows out at 90°; shoulders complain | Tuck toward ~45°; wrists stacked over elbows |
| Rib flare / soft core | Low back arches off the bench/floor | Ribs down; brace like a light punch is coming |
| Bounce & heave | Momentum at the bottom | Control the lower; pause a beat when needed |
| Hard lockout | Elbows slam every rep | Soft finish — tension stays on |
| Shrugged shoulders | Necks shorten; bells drift | Pack blades into the bench/floor before press one |

Floor press is a friend if shoulders feel crowded on a full bench ROM — still practice, still Bulk-worthy.

### One-arm / supported row

**Blueprint:** Hinge or bench-support brace; pull the **elbow** toward the hip/rib, not a wrist curl. Spine stays honest; torso doesn’t twist for the last inch.

**Common misses**

| Mistake | What it looks like | Better cue |
|---------|-------------------|------------|
| Torso twist | Hips/shoulders rotate to “finish” | Brace harder; stop when the elbow stacks — no twist |
| Shrug row | Trap does the work; neck rises | Pull elbow to pocket; keep neck long |
| Rounded start | Soft hinge before the first pull | Set the hinge first; then grab |
| Yanking | Bounce at the bottom | Dead-stop or controlled stretch — no heave |
| Half ROM | Elbow barely clears the ribs | Own the squeeze at the top; full lower under control |

## 3. Our three templates

Tied to the five-phase model. Times are approximate.

### Goblet Squat Path · ~35–45 min

**Purpose:** Own an upright, honest front-loaded squat you can load later.  
**Who it’s for:** Day-one lifters, home-gym folks, anyone cleaning up a tippy squat before a barbell.  
**Session shape**

| Phase | What you do |
|-------|-------------|
| Warm-up | Ankle rocks, hip openers, empty squat, light goblet |
| Skill drills | Pause goblets (light), heel-down drills, brace breath |
| Form | Goblet squat @ working load — quality sets |
| **THE BULK** | Higher-volume goblets · **RPE target 7–8** |
| Cool-down | Fold, 90/90, breathe |

Default when this module opens first: **Goblet Squat Path**.

### Press Path · ~35–45 min

**Purpose:** Groove a packed, quiet press — floor or bench.  
**Who it’s for:** After the brace feels honest; people building push strength without ego flare.  
**Session shape**

| Phase | What you do |
|-------|-------------|
| Warm-up | Shoulder CARs, scap sets, light floor press |
| Skill drills | Pause at chest, tempo lowers, soft lock practice |
| Form | Presses @ working load — quality sets |
| **THE BULK** | Press density sets · **RPE target 7–8** |
| Cool-down | Shoulder CARs, doorway openers, breathe |

### Row Path · ~35–45 min

**Purpose:** Build a strong pull that doesn’t twist or shrug.  
**Who it’s for:** Desk-day posture seekers, push-heavy lifters balancing the week, anyone prepping for pull-ups later.  
**Session shape**

| Phase | What you do |
|-------|-------------|
| Warm-up | Hinge pattern, band pull-aparts, light deadlift |
| Skill drills | Elbow-to-hip rows (light), isometric squeezes |
| Form | Supported / one-arm rows @ working load |
| **THE BULK** | Row density · **RPE target 7–8** |
| Cool-down | Thoracic openers, child’s pose, breathe |

## 4. RPE & The Bulk — why we require 1–10

Same product rule as kettlebell (`MVP_IA.md`):

| Number | Plain English |
|--------|----------------|
| 1–3 | Easy / warm-up feel |
| 4–5 | Working, conversation still fine |
| 6–7 | Focused; quality still high |
| 7–8 | Hard but clean — typical Bulk target here |
| 9 | Almost max; little left |
| 10 | Nothing left (we don’t chase this every day) |

**Why required on The Bulk?** Main stimulus lives here; readiness changes day to day; practice culture prefers quality over grinding to a pile; one shared language for history on **You**. Optional RPE elsewhere; **required before you leave The Bulk.**

## 5. Gear (high-level)

Adjustable pair or a small fixed set both work. Hex / rubber heads are kinder to floors.

**Practical starting map** (community norms — adjust for *your* strength, not a label):

| If this is you | Common early pair |
|----------------|-------------------|
| Newer / lighter presser | ~5–10 lb / 2–5 kg per hand for skill; slightly heavier for goblet |
| Average home gym | ~15–25 lb / 7–12 kg pair to start compounds |
| Stronger returner | Start where form stays honest — often lighter than “ego memory” |

**Notes for Structr templates**

- **Goblet** uses **one** bell — often heavier than a single press dumbbell.  
- **Press / row** usually want a matched pair.  
- One lighter pair for skill + one heavier for Bulk beats one tiny set you’ll outgrow in a week.  
- Bench optional — floor press is valid day-one.

Clear floor, stable footing, space to fail a press safely (no face-crush setups with max ego).

## 6. FAQ

**Is this bodybuilding or practice?**  
Practice first. You’ll get stronger — the point is better squats, presses, and rows, not max sweat.

**Which template first?**  
**Goblet Squat Path**, unless you already own a clean squat and want press or pull emphasis.

**Do I need a bench?**  
No. Floor press covers Press Path day one.

**Do I need RPE on every phase?**  
No. **Required on The Bulk** only.

**What if form falls apart mid-Bulk?**  
Park early. Lighter next time beats unfinished sloppy sets.

**Can I reorder phases?**  
Not in MVP. Five-phase order stays fixed.

**How does this relate to kettlebell?**  
Same logging model. Goblet pattern overlaps kettlebell goblet warm-ups; this module is the **dumbbell skill** home under **Get stronger**.

**Where do I go when technique is fuzzy?**  
Info cues + film from the side (squat) / 45° (press) / side (row). A coach when you can.
